The safest approach is:

Add fields now only when nearly every entity will eventually use them.
Use optional structs for specialised mechanics.
Use callbacks for genuinely different behaviour.
Use enums for stable categories, not individual behaviours.
My strongest recommendations to add early
Location	Suggested field	Purpose
Enemy identity	tags: []	Flexible filtering such as "organic", "mechanical", "drone", "capital", "boss_add" without creating endless enums.
Enemy stats	mass	Gameplay mass for knockback, collision impulse and gravitational effects. Keep separate from visual_mass.
Enemy identity	threat_value	Encounter generation, target prioritisation and difficulty estimates.
Enemy reward	drop_table_key	Future items, ore, components and rare drops without embedding drop logic in every enemy.
Enemy combat	status_resistance	Resistance to disruption, burn, corrosion and stagger.

Of these, I would only implement mass, threat_value, tags, and perhaps drop_table_key immediately. Everything else can wait until its mechanic arrives.

Feedback priority system

Eventually classify feedback into three rough priorities:

Minor: ordinary bullets, basic pickups.
Major: rockets, armour breaks, elite deaths.
Critical: beam lock, boss attacks, player hull breach.

This prevents the screen from becoming equally noisy for every event. It does not necessarily need an enum yet; a simple numeric priority is more flexible.

Your present structure is healthy. The next universally useful additions I would personally choose are:

Gameplay mass
Controls physical behaviour: knockback resistance, collision force, recoil push and future gravity effects. Separate from visual_mass, which only affects thruster appearance.
Enemy threat_value
A difficulty score used when generating encounters. For example, a sector with a 20-point budget could spawn ten 2-point fighters or two 10-point elites.
Identity tags
Flexible labels such as "mechanical", "drone" or "capital". Systems can filter enemies by tags without needing new enums or hardcoded checks.
Reward drop_table_key
Points to a reusable loot table defining possible item and resource drops. Multiple enemies can share one table while retaining different credit rewards.
Damage hit position/direction
Stores exactly where an attack landed and which direction it came from. Useful for correctly positioned sparks, debris, knockback, armour damage and directional indicators.
Faction feedback colours
Centralizes each faction’s alert, targeting, telegraph, shield and warning colours. All ships from that faction automatically share a consistent visual language.
Projectile danger_level
Tells feedback systems how seriously to present a projectile. Ordinary bullets might receive no warning, while rockets and boss attacks receive indicators, sounds or targeting brackets.

Those seven additions open a lot of future gameplay without forcing unfinished mechanics to run or adding substantial per-step overhead.
